---
HUMAN NEUROANATOMY

1. ANATOMICAL PLANES AND POSITIONS
   - Sagittal Plane: Divides body into left and right halves
   - Coronal Plane: Divides body into front and back
   - Transverse Plane: Divides body into superior and inferior parts

2. ANATOMICAL DIRECTIONS
   - Ventral (Anterior): Front side of the body
   - Dorsal (Posterior): Back side of the body
   - Cranial (Superior): Toward the head
   - Caudal (Inferior): Toward the tailbone
   - Medial: Toward the midline
   - Lateral: Away from the midline
   - Proximal: Closer to the center/origin of limb
   - Distal: Further from the center/origin of limb
   - Radial: Toward the thumb side of forearm
   - Ulnar: Toward the pinky side of forearm

3. SPINAL NERVE DISTRIBUTION
   - Cervical Spine (C1-C8): Neck, shoulders, arms
   - Thoracic Spine (T1-T12): Mid-back, ribs
   - Lumbar Spine (L1-L5): Lower back, legs
   - Sacral Spine (S1-S5): Pelvis, lower limbs
   - Coccygeal: Tail region

4. COMMON ANATOMICAL LANDMARKS
   - Vertebra Prominens (C7): Most prominent cervical vertebra
   - Acromion Process: Highest point of shoulder
   - ASIS (Anterior Superior Iliac Spine): Front pelvic landmark
   - PSIS (Posterior Superior Iliac Spine): Back pelvic landmark

---
CARDIOVASCULAR SYSTEM EXAMINATION

1. VITAL SIGNS ASSESSMENT
   - Heart Rate (HR): Normal 60-100 bpm at rest
   - Blood Pressure (BP): Normal <120/80 mmHg
   - Respiratory Rate (RR): Normal 12-20 breaths/min
   - Temperature: Normal 36.5-37.5°C (98.6-99.5°F)
   - Oxygen Saturation (SpO2): Normal >95% on room air

2. CARDIAC AUSCULTATION
   - S1 (First Heart Sound): Lub - closure of AV valves (mitral, tricuspid)
   - S2 (Second Heart Sound): Dub - closure of semilunar valves (aortic, pulmonic)
   - Normal rate: Regular rhythm, S1 & S2 clearly heard
   - Murmurs: Abnormal turbulent blood flow sounds
   - Gallops: S3 (ventricular filling) or S4 (atrial contraction)

3. PULSE ASSESSMENT
   - Rate: Normal 60-100 bpm
   - Rhythm: Regular or regularly irregular
   - Amplitude: Strength of pulse (bounding vs weak)
   - Laterality: Compare left vs right side symmetry

4. CHEST PAIN EVALUATION (RED FLAGS)
   - Acute coronary syndrome: Chest pressure/heaviness, radiation to arm/jaw
   - Tachycardia: HR >100 bpm at rest
   - Diaphoresis: Excessive sweating
   - Dyspnea: Shortness of breath
   - Hypotension: Systolic BP <90 mmHg

CATEGORY: Neurology
SCENARIO_TAGS: stroke, aphasia, neuro_exam, speech_pathology
CLINICAL_CONTEXT: Neurological assessment and stroke evaluation

---
STROKE AND NEUROLOGICAL DEFICIT

1. STROKE RECOGNITION (FAST TEST)
   - F (Face): Ask to smile, check for asymmetry
   - A (Arm): Raise both arms, check for drift
   - S (Speech): Repeat simple phrase, check for slurring
   - T (Time): Note exact time of symptom onset

2. APHASIA TYPES AND CHARACTERISTICS
   - Broca's Aphasia: Non-fluent, expressive difficulty, comprehension preserved
     * Speech effortful and slow
     * Telegraphic speech (short phrases, mostly content words)
     * Repetition often impaired
     * Comprehension relatively intact
   
   - Wernicke's Aphasia: Fluent but meaningless, comprehension severe impairment
     * Speech flows easily but lacks meaning
     * Paraphasias (sound/word substitutions)
     * Severe comprehension deficit
     * Repetition impaired

3. COMMON STROKE SYMPTOMS
   - Unilateral weakness (hemiparesis)
   - Facial droop (asymmetry)
   - Speech difficulty (dysarthria, aphasia)
   - Vision loss (monocular or field cut)
   - Ataxia (loss of coordination)
   - Vertigo (room spinning sensation)

4. STROKE TIMELINE (CRITICAL FOR TREATMENT)
   - Time = Brain: Every minute ~190,000 neurons die
   - Thrombolytic window: 0-4.5 hours from symptom onset (critical)
   - Mechanical thrombectomy: 0-24 hours (with imaging evidence)
   - Post-stroke care: 24-72 hours assessment and rehabilitation

DOCUMENT ID: ORTHO_001
CATEGORY: Orthopedics & Physiotherapy
SCENARIO_TAGS: physiotherapy, back_pain, neck_pain, musculoskeletal, ergonomics
CLINICAL_CONTEXT: Musculoskeletal assessment and physiotherapy

---
MUSCULOSKELETAL ASSESSMENT

1. NECK PAIN (CERVICAL SYNDROME) - COMMON PRESENTATION
   - Patient Profile: Often office workers, prolonged sitting
   - Classic Symptoms:
     * Neck stiffness, limited range of motion
     * Headaches (cervicogenic origin)
     * Referred pain to shoulders/arms
     * Possible paresthesia (numbness/tingling)
   
   - Ergonomic Risk Factors:
     * Monitor height too low (causes forward head posture)
     * Desk height inappropriate for arm position
     * Lack of micro-breaks during work
     * Poor chair support

2. LOWER BACK PAIN (LUMBAR SYNDROME)
   - Red Flags Requiring Urgent Imaging:
     * Cauda equina syndrome: bilateral leg pain, saddle anesthesia, bladder loss
     * Fracture: trauma history, osteoporosis, sudden onset
     * Malignancy: night pain, weight loss, age >50
     * Infection: fever, IV drug use, immunosuppression
   
   - Radiculopathy Pattern (L4/L5 nerve root involvement):
     * Pain radiates down LATERAL leg/calf
     * Weakness in dorsiflexion (foot drop)
     * Numbness in foot dorsum
     * Diminished ankle reflex

3. ASSESSMENT PROTOCOL
   - Range of Motion (ROM) Testing:
     * Flexion: Touch toes (normal near 0-10cm)
     * Extension: Arch backward (normal 20-30°)
     * Lateral flexion: Bend sideways (normal 15-20°)
     * Rotation: Turn shoulders (normal 40-45°/side)
   
   - Special Tests:
     * Straight Leg Raise (SLR): Radiculopathy if pain <60°
     * Crossed SLR: Higher specificity for nerve root compression
     * Palpation: Identify tender points, trigger points

4. WORKPLACE ERGONOMICS ADVICE
   - Monitor: Eye level (15° below horizontal)
   - Keyboard/Mouse: Elbows 90° flexion
   - Chair: Hip slightly higher than knees, armrest support
   - Breaks: Every 30min, 5min movement break
   - Posture: Neutral spine (natural curves maintained)

DOCUMENT ID: SPEECH_001
CATEGORY: Speech-Language Pathology
SCENARIO_TAGS: speech_pathology, logopedics, aphasia, communication_disorders
CLINICAL_CONTEXT: Speech and language assessment

---
LANGUAGE DEVELOPMENT AND DISORDERS

1. TYPICAL LANGUAGE DEVELOPMENT (CHILDREN)
   - 0-3 months: Cooing sounds
   - 3-6 months: Babbling (da-da, ba-ba)
   - 6-12 months: First words ("mama", "dada")
   - 12-18 months: 50 word vocabulary, two-word combinations emerging
   - 18-24 months: 50-100+ words, simple sentences
   - 2-3 years: 500+ words, grammar developing
   - 3-5 years: Complex sentences, story-telling ability

2. AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER (ASD) - RED FLAGS
   - Language Delays:
     * Limited vocabulary for age
     * Echolalia (repeating words without understanding)
     * Pronoun reversal ("you" for "I")
     * Limited gesture use (pointing, waving)
   
   - Social Communication Issues:
     * Limited eye contact
     * Difficulty with joint attention
     * Unusual prosody (speech melody/rhythm)
     * Limited interest in peer interaction

3. LATE TALKER vs LANGUAGE DISORDER
   - Late Talker (Better Prognosis):
     * Age 18-30 months, <50 words
     * Normal comprehension
     * Good non-verbal skills
     * Typical play development
   
   - Language Disorder (Needs Intervention):
     * Age 24+ months with significant delay
     * Comprehension also impaired
     * Limited gesture/non-verbal communication
     * Unusual play patterns

4. ASSESSMENT QUESTIONS FOR CAREGIVERS
   - Does child understand simple directions ("Get your shoes")?
   - Does child point to objects of interest?
   - Does child make eye contact during interaction?
   - Does child use gestures (waving, pointing)?
   - How many words does child use?
   - Does child combine words (2+ word phrases)?
   - Does child engage in pretend play (playing house, etc)?

DOCUMENT ID: MIDWIFERY_001
CATEGORY: Obstetrics & Midwifery
SCENARIO_TAGS: midwifery, postpartum, lactation, neonatal
CLINICAL_CONTEXT: Postpartum assessment and newborn care

---
POSTPARTUM ASSESSMENT AND NEONATAL CARE

1. POSTPARTUM MOTHER - EARLY ASSESSMENT (24-72 HOURS)
   - Vital Signs Monitoring:
     * Heart Rate: May be elevated 80-100 bpm (fluid shifts)
     * Blood Pressure: Should normalize to pre-pregnancy levels
     * Temperature: Fever >38°C suggests infection
     * Lochia: Vaginal discharge - normal progression: rubra→serosa→alba
   
   - Episiotomy/Wound Healing:
     * Mild erythema normal first 24h
     * Excessive swelling/redness suggests infection
     * Significant pain warrants evaluation
     * Assess for dehiscence (opening)

2. BREASTFEEDING SUPPORT
   - Common Issues & Solutions:
     * Engorgement: Warm compress before feeding, cold after
     * Sore Nipples: Correct latch is crucial, lanolin/hydrogel pads
     * Low Supply: Frequent feeds (8-12x/day), adequate hydration
     * Mastitis: Fever, localized redness/pain - antibiotic treatment
   
   - When to Suggest "Abpumpen" (Pumping):
     * Mother needs rest but supply to maintain
     * Sore nipples preventing direct feeding
     * Working outside home requiring milk expression
     * Oversupply causing engorgement

3. NEWBORN ASSESSMENTS (FIRST 24-48 HOURS)
   - Jaundice Screening:
     * Physiologic jaundice: Appears after 24h
     * Bilirubin levels: <5mg/dL day 1, <15mg/dL day 3
     * Risk factors: Prematurity, hemolytic disease, poor feeding
   
   - Weight Patterns:
     * Birth weight baseline
     * 5-10% loss normal in first 3-5 days
     * Recovery to birth weight by day 10-14
     * Poor feeding pattern if >10% loss

4. FEEDING MODE COUNSELING
   - Breastfeeding Benefits:
     * Optimal nutrition and immunity building
     * Mother-baby bonding and oxytocin benefits
     * Cost-effective, always available
   
   - When Additional Feeding May Be Needed:
     * Excessive weight loss (>10%)
     * Poor latch despite support
     * Low maternal supply (rare initially)
     * Medical conditions (maternal infection, neonatal hypoglycemia)

DOCUMENT ID: EMERGENCY_001
CATEGORY: Emergency Medicine
SCENARIO_TAGS: paramedic, emergency_medicine, trauma, ABCDE, SAMPLER
CLINICAL_CONTEXT: Emergency assessment protocols

---
EMERGENCY ASSESSMENT PROTOCOLS

1. PRIMARY SURVEY - ABCDE APPROACH
   - A (Airway): Is it patent? Clear obstruction?
   - B (Breathing): Respiratory rate, effort, SpO2?
   - C (Circulation): Pulse present? Hemorrhage control?
   - D (Disability): Consciousness level = GCS score
   - E (Exposure): Undress to examine, prevent hypothermia

2. SECONDARY SURVEY - SAMPLER HISTORY
   - S (Symptoms): What does patient report?
   - A (Allergies): Medication allergies documented?
   - M (Medications): Current medications taken?
   - P (Past Medical History): Relevant conditions?
   - L (Last Meal): When did they last eat/drink?
   - E (Events): What led to this incident?
   - R (Reason): Chief complaint in patient's words

3. CONSCIOUSNESS ASSESSMENT - GLASGOW COMA SCALE (GCS)
   - Eye Opening (E): 1-4 points
     * 4: Spontaneous
     * 3: To verbal command
     * 2: To pain
     * 1: No response
   
   - Verbal Response (V): 1-5 points
     * 5: Oriented and conversing
     * 4: Disoriented conversation
     * 3: Inappropriate words
     * 2: Incomprehensible sounds
     * 1: No response
   
   - Motor Response (M): 1-6 points
     * 6: Obeys commands
     * 5: Localizes pain
     * 4: Withdraws from pain
     * 3: Abnormal flexion (decorticate)
     * 2: Abnormal extension (decerebrate)
     * 1: No response
   
   - Score Interpretation:
     * Severe: GCS ≤8 (intubation consideration)
     * Moderate: GCS 9-12
     * Mild: GCS 13-15

4. TRAUMA ASSESSMENT COMMON SCENARIOS
   - Bike Fall Impact Pattern:
     * Assess for head injury (neurological signs)
     * Rib fractures (pain on inspiration, possible pneumothorax)
     * Limb injury (neurovascular integrity)
     * Abdominal injury (tenderness, rigidity)
   
   - Red Flags Requiring Immediate Admission:
     * Loss of consciousness
     * Severe head/chest/abdominal pain
     * Neurological deficits
     * Significant deformity
